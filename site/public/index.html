DAORework

A mod manager, launcher and diagnostics tool for Dragon Age: Origins.

Preview first. Back up always. Undo anything. Free, always.

What it does

  • Manages the mods you already installed by hand, without reinstalling them. Official DLC is never touched.
  • Moves your mods over from Vortex. Move from Vortex takes you through it a step at a time: copy them, purge in Vortex, then turn them on in the same order. It has only been run on simulated Vortex records so far, and doesn't cover add-ins Vortex placed yet.
  • Picks up new downloads, if you turn it on. When you come back from your browser, the mods you downloaded are offered on the Mods page. Nothing is added without a click.
  • Adds mods safely. Add a .dazip, .zip, .7z or .rar. Turning a mod on shows a preview of exactly what will be copied, replaced or removed, and nothing changes until you apply it. A backup is taken first.
  • Finds the mod behind a problem. Find the problem mod turns off half your mods, you play and say whether the problem is still there, and it repeats until one is left: about 8 rounds for 137 mods. What changed shows what's new since your last game with other mods, with a way back.
  • Keeps one copy of each mod. Adding a mod looks first and offers to take over what you already have, and Keep one of each clears the copies a library builds up, from one preview. Removed copies go to the Recycle Bin.
  • Shows conflicts, most serious first. For every file two mods share, see whose copy the game uses and what that means, and arrange them. What could break quests comes before what only changes how things look. Files inside .erf archives are compared too, though there DAORework can't tell which copy the game uses.
  • Hair, head and colour mods work together. When several mods ship the character creator's chargenmorphcfg.xml, DAORework combines them, so every mod's hairs, heads and colours show.
  • Checks every mod. Your own antivirus scans it before it is kept, programs inside a mod are never installed, and a download that differs from the official one is pointed out.
  • Turns on DXVK, and the 4 GB patch on copies that allow it (GOG, EA App). Steam's own executable is protected and Steam will not start it once patched, so DAORework leaves the Steam copy alone and tells you if it was patched before. It notices when Steam's Verify integrity has undone a fix.
  • Checks your game and suggests what is worth a look, and backs up your saves. Home says whether the game is ready to play, and what to do next, including when your mods are in a documents folder the game doesn't read. History lists every change, each with Undo to here.

It never downloads mods or game files, and never sends anything over the internet. Everything stays on your computer.

See it

Download

Windows 10 or 11, 64-bit, and Dragon Age: Origins (any edition). Nothing else to install.

Download on Nexus Mods Latest release on GitHub

Both are the same zip. Unzip it anywhere and run DAORework.exe.

A new version may be held on Nexus for a day or two. DAORework contains programs, so Nexus staff check each new upload by hand before it can be downloaded there. The same zip is on GitHub straight away, and you can check your download.

Check your download

From 0.3.1, DAORework.exe and dao.exe are code-signed: right-click either one, choose Properties, then Digital Signatures to see the publisher. A new publisher has to build up reputation with Windows, so SmartScreen may still say it protected your PC on the first releases. If it does, choose More info, then Run anyway, after checking the zip one of these ways:

How it is built

DAORework is designed and directed by Amber Rastella, with an AI coding assistant writing much of the code under strict engineering standards, written decisions, automated tests and a run on a real install before every release. The whole story, in her own words: How DAORework is built.
